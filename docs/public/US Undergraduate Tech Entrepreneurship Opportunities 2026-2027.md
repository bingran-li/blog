# US Undergraduate Tech Entrepreneurship Opportunities — Cornell to California

**Research date:** September 1, 2026  
**Profile used:** Cornell undergraduate, expected graduation 2029; Systems Engineering + Computer Science; U.S. citizen; almost 19; strong application/prototyping skills; three immature MVPs; no incorporated company; team, users, revenue and prior funding not assumed.

## Bottom line

The best near-term strategy is **not fundraising first**. It is to select one MVP, obtain credible user evidence, and run structured cofounder trials while retaining the option to stay enrolled. His ability to code means the default complement is not automatically “a CTO.” For a conventional software product, a cofounder with unusual customer access, domain knowledge, distribution or sales ability may be more valuable. An ML cofounder becomes important only if proprietary modeling, evaluation, data or inference architecture is central to the product. If “networking” means a social/professional network, prioritize growth, community and marketplace design; if it means computer networking, prioritize distributed-systems/security depth. This must be resolved before matching.

Do **not incorporate merely to look serious**. Cornell's BIG Idea Competition excludes ideas already operating as a business or placed in a legal entity, while YC does not require incorporation to apply. Incorporate when an investment, contract, liability or IP reason makes it necessary, with legal advice.

Status labels used below:

- **① Open/current:** an application, contact route or event is usable now.
- **② 2027 published:** official 2027 dates or terms are published.
- **③ Recurring; 2027 pending:** prior/current-cycle facts exist, but 2027 dates are not announced.
- **④ Closed/ineligible/unverified:** the relevant cycle is closed, eligibility does not fit, or a material term cannot be verified.

## Five highest-priority opportunities

### 1. Cornell founder network: Eship Kickoff → eHub → Launchpad Ezra

**Why it fits:** This is the fastest, lowest-risk route to identify Cornell cofounders, mentors, student investors and customer introductions without leaving school. The September 3 Eship Kickoff includes more than 20 campus entrepreneurship programs and other students building companies. eHub is open to pre-company concepts; Launchpad Ezra offers bookable founder mentors at any stage.

**Problem solved:** Cofounder pipeline, MVP selection, accountability and warm introductions. It is a relationship-building channel—not funding by itself.

**Largest constraint:** Quality depends on repeated participation and specific asks. “Looking for a CTO” is too vague; bring a one-page description of the chosen user problem and a two-week trial project.

**Next action:** Attend [Eship Kickoff on September 3, 4:30–6:30 p.m. at eHub Collegetown](https://eship.cornell.edu/all-events/entrepreneurship-kickoff/), request [eHub 24/7 access](https://eship.cornell.edu/item/ehub/), and book one [Launchpad Ezra mentor](https://eship.cornell.edu/launchpad-ezra-mentors/) in customer discovery or go-to-market. Ask at Kickoff whether 2026 Startup Hours dates and any eLab late/waitlist route have been released.

### 2. YC Co-Founder Matching

**Why it fits:** It is free, online, open to people with or without an idea, and designed for both immediate and future cofounder searches. Profiles are visible only to approved participants.

**Problem solved:** Expands the search beyond Cornell and allows filtering for a domain/GTM, ML/data or systems complement.

**Largest constraint:** A match is not diligence. A polished profile or good conversation does not establish commitment, judgment or working compatibility.

**Next action:** Create a [YC Co-Founder Matching](https://www.ycombinator.com/cofounder-matching) profile around one user problem, not three unrelated MVPs. Invite 10 candidates; run two 14-day build-and-customer-interview trials before discussing final titles or equity.

### 3. Marla & Barry Beck and Ezra Startup Summer Program — Summer 2027

**Why it fits:** This is a confirmed, non-dilutive Cornell option for spending Summer 2027 on his own startup while remaining a student. It offers up to **$6,000 per person or company team**, and the funds may be used for compensation, prototypes, legal work or web development.

**Problem solved:** Buys concentrated founder time for product iteration, customer discovery and team building.

**Largest constraint:** Recipients must spend **90% of the summer** on the startup. The program prefers evidence of entrepreneurship involvement and ideally some traction. $6,000 is unlikely to cover an entire summer in San Francisco by itself; location permission and California use should be confirmed.

**Next action:** Work backward from the [February 19, 2027 deadline](https://eship.cornell.edu/item/beck-ezra-startup-summer/): by December, target one MVP, 30+ user conversations, a functioning demo and either repeated usage, a pilot/LOI, or a clearly documented failed assumption and pivot.

### 4. Neo Scholars / Neo Residency — prepare for the 2027 cycle

**Why it fits:** Neo explicitly targets strong college CS students and future founders. In the 2026 structure, student teams received a **$40,000 equity-free grant per student**, three months of SF workspace, mentorship and recruiting access; students were not required to drop out or incorporate. This directly combines a technical peer network, cofounder access and a California summer.

**Problem solved:** California founder network, focused building time, mentorship, startup recruiting fallback and investor exposure.

**Largest constraint:** **2027 has not been announced** and the 2026 application is closed. The public Scholars page does not expose a detailed graduation-year rule; confirm when the next application opens. The all-expenses-paid coverage described by Neo applies to its Oregon bootcamp and Arizona Demo Day trip; ordinary SF housing is not stated as covered.

**Next action:** Join the [Neo Scholars](https://portal.neo.com/scholars) update path and monitor [Neo Residency](https://neo.com/residency). Prepare a concise technical portfolio and a demo with user evidence rather than trying to imitate AI research credentials he does not have.

### 5. PearX — W27 is open; S27 should be monitored

**Why it fits:** PearX accepts solo founders, idea-stage companies and teams that have not raised. It can help find a cofounder or founding hire, win first customers, and prepare fundraising. **W27 applications are open through October 4, 2026.**

**Problem solved:** Structured customer discovery, recruiting, first sales and a direct institutional-investor relationship.

**Largest constraint:** All teams must relocate to the SF Bay Area for 12 weeks. W27 runs January–April, so participation would conflict with a Cornell semester. Pear invests **$500,000–$2 million**, but the public page does not state a standard valuation/equity percentage; this is investment, not a grant. Housing is not stated as covered.

**Next action:** Treat [PearX W27](https://pear.vc/pearx/) as a stretch only if he is prepared to work full-time and consider a leave. Otherwise, ask Pear when S27 will open and use October 4 as a forcing date to produce the application materials without necessarily submitting an under-evidenced company.

## Next 30 days

### Days 1–7: choose the problem, not the pitch

1. Write a one-page card for each MVP: exact user, painful job, current workaround, why now, fastest falsification test, and what evidence already exists.
2. Conduct **five user interviews per MVP**. Do not demo first; ask about recent behavior, spending, failed workarounds and decision authority.
3. Score each MVP on pain frequency, reachable users, speed to pilot, founder insight, retention mechanism, regulatory/capital burden and willingness to pay. Select one by September 7.
4. Attend Eship Kickoff with a specific ask: “I need a collaborator who has X customer access / ML-evaluation depth / security expertise for a two-week sprint.”

### Days 8–14: produce evidence and meet collaborators

1. Ship one narrow workflow for the selected MVP and instrument activation, repeat use and failure points.
2. Recruit 10 cofounder candidates across Cornell and YC Matching; screen for values, ambition, availability, problem insight and conflict style—not title.
3. Start two parallel 14-day trials with scoped outputs: five joint interviews, one product change, one sales/outreach experiment and a written retrospective.
4. Book one Launchpad mentor for customer discovery and one with the relevant domain/GTM background.

### Days 15–21: decide programs from evidence

1. Target a cumulative **20–30 interviews**, 5–10 active testers and at least one concrete pilot/LOI/payment request. An LOI is evidence of interest, not revenue.
2. If the project is VentureWell-eligible, finish PI and institutional approvals. If not, do not contort ordinary SaaS into a hardware/impact grant application.
3. Draft PearX, YC, DRF and Neo-style answers, but decide separately whether to submit and whether the commitment terms fit.
4. Apply to 1517 Medici with a precise use of funds; contact DRF for feedback only when the selected MVP story is coherent.

### Days 22–30: lock one direction and a summer option

1. End cofounder trials with a written decision on values, role coverage, time commitment, equity expectations and a 90-day plan. No cofounder is better than a mismatched one.
2. Publish a 90-second demo and a one-page evidence memo. Update metrics weekly.
3. Submit VentureWell by September 29 if eligible; make the PearX W27 go/no-go decision before October 4.
4. Apply to current Summer 2027 SF startup roles as insurance and create a calendar for Neo, Beck/Ezra, YC S27 and later eLab/competition cycles.

## Reusable application materials

### Required now

- **Founder brief (150–250 words):** what he has built, why this user problem, relevant technical/product evidence, and what he personally owns. Do not equate strong speaking with proven CEO execution.
- **One-sentence company description:** user + painful job + product outcome; avoid “AI-powered platform” unless AI is essential.
- **90-second product demo:** real workflow, real or clearly labeled sample data, current limitations.
- **User-evidence memo:** interview count, participant definition, verbatim insights kept short, current workaround, activation/repeat-use data, pilot/payment status and disconfirming evidence.
- **MVP selection scorecard:** why this idea beat the other two and which assumptions remain risky.
- **Cofounder specification:** required capability, why it is founder-level rather than an employee/advisor need, expected time, decision rights and trial plan.
- **Light 8–10 slide deck:** problem, user, current alternatives, demo, insight, evidence, market wedge, team, next milestones and use of funds.
- **Links:** GitHub/technical portfolio, live demo, short video, LinkedIn and a simple landing page.

### Prepare after traction or before accepting money

- Incorporation documents, founder stock/vesting, IP-assignment agreements and cap table.
- Detailed financial model, hiring plan and 18-month use-of-funds plan.
- Customer reference list, signed pilots/contracts, privacy/security documentation and data room.
- ML evaluation set/model-cost analysis only if ML performance is central.
- Counsel-reviewed SAFE/equity comparison for YC, Pear, Antler, DRF, Neo-startup terms or 1517 investment. Service credits are not cash and should be listed separately.

## Five questions that would most change the ranking

1. What does each of the three MVPs do, who is the first user/buyer, and does “networking” mean social/professional networking or computer networking?
2. For each MVP, how many real users, interviews, repeat users, pilots, LOIs and dollars of revenue exist today?
3. Is anyone already building or selling with him, and what have they shipped together?
4. Which founder-level gap is genuinely hardest: domain/customer access, sales/distribution, ML/data, security/distributed systems, design/product, or something else?
5. What cash budget can he personally commit to Summer 2027 travel/housing, and would he consider a semester leave only after acceptance/funding, or is staying enrolled a firm constraint?
